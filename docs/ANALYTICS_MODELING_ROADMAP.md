## Data adequacy and first real pilot

The input is two public research releases (`analysis-evidence-2026-10-12`, `analysis-evidence-2026-10-13`) from the project's own original NASA FIRMS archive. The repaired importer verified **5 scan receipts**, **10 original pixel assets**, source SHA-256 hashes and row provenance. Across the two releases, 1,351,891 pixel rows were read; 994,444 were repeated by source record identity, leaving **357,447 unique original raw pixels** in SQLite. The 12 fixed, nonoverlapping 250 km circles contain **26,205 unique raw pixels**; 331,242 lie outside. Observed UTC dates run **2026-10-09 through 2026-10-13**, but the first scan is a rolling 72-hour bootstrap, and no day has proven continuous satellite acquisition. The regions are an exploratory selection, not a random or representative worldwide sample.

The first capture scans are at 2026-10-12 18:19, 2026-10-13 00:20, 06:35, 12:50 and 18:50 UTC. Selected-region *new* candidate cell-hour counts per scan are **7,194, 447, 0, 0, 1,329** respectively. The middle zeros describe no *newly archived* selected candidates, not no fire, no satellite anomaly, or no satellite overpass. Source query receipts are complete for both VIIRS sources in these scans; overpass/exposure coverage is unverified. Annual and seasonal comparisons, a prevalence denominator, incident outcomes, environment correlations and fire-rate trends are unavailable.

The experiment's target is next scan's newly archived candidate cell-hour count in a selected circle. It skips the bootstrap transition, trains a one-lag persistence baseline and log1p single-feature OLS on **20 earlier region transitions**, and reserves **10 later temporal**, **4 earlier geographic**, and **2 joint temporal/geographic** transitions. Australia North and Brazil Amazon are geographic holdouts. Training targets contain one value (zero): fitted intercept and slope are both zero. On the temporal holdout, persistence MAE = **131.8** and log-linear MAE = **131.8** candidate cell-hours; on the joint holdout both MAEs = **5.5** (n=2). Geographic holdout MAEs are 68.0 and 0.0 respectively (n=4), but a constant-zero fit and sparse acquisitions make the apparent improvement meaningless. **No model beats the baseline with supportable scientific evidence.** There are no uncertainty intervals or calibrated fire probabilities. This is executable research plumbing, not a usable forecast.

The compact aggregate report is [the fixed pilot JSON](../public/data/thermal-capture-pilot-2026-10-13.json) (updated after the eligibility contract; SHA-256 `baa401b07c06c48868e491ee9d4ad9e4ae85cf8eb54afa89653c161833189285`). The report pins all scan receipt hashes and the 10 pixel asset hashes/row counts. A second import directly from the original downloaded assets reproduced the complete JSON byte for byte and the same SHA-256. Raw pixels and SQLite remain outside Git. Local research transfer used about 129 MB compressed release assets and 240 MB SQLite; neither enters Workers KV or Cloudflare. The browser reads static aggregate JSON, with no new service, secret or paid quota.

## 2026-10-14 execution: durability, weather and eligibility

The [Antalya 2023–2025 study](research/antalya-annual-thermal-weather-2023-2025.md) extends the observation window with official standard-processing NASA FIRMS country CSVs, distinct from the 2026 NRT receipt archive. Six country files contain **257,907** original rows and **1,340** raw pixels within 25 km of the NOAA GHCNh Antalya airport station. Three genuine station-year files contain **62,230** source rows. Of **994** positive sensor × UTC-hour groups, **988** have an earlier temperature observation within 60 minutes. Monthly and yearly counts, sensor comparisons, fixed-station medians and missingness are available in the web UI; no negative hours, satellite acquisition denominator, independent fire outcome or decision-time availability is inferred. The annual builder streams the country files and uses disk SQLite for exact source-row deduplication; it caps selected pixels and reads one station-year at a time. The nine raw files (67,856,657 bytes) remain outside Git, while the source-hashed 529,006-byte analytical JSON is static on Pages. The published file replayed byte for byte from the pinned originals. This is a substantial retrospective descriptive pilot, not a weather-association regression or fire forecast; a 2025 count drop cannot be called a fire trend without exposure evidence.

The [Dalaman 2023–2025 study](research/dalaman-annual-thermal-weather-2023-2025.md) reuses the **same six NASA hashes** and preselects a second NOAA station-centered 25 km circle from the official station list before inspecting counts. Three new GHCNh station-year files contribute **61,539** source rows. The circle contains **79** archived raw pixels and **38** positive sensor-hours, all 38 with a qualifying prior temperature. The separate browser reports show year-by-region, source-specific counts and missingness without a fire-rate ranking. Its 48,003-byte report also replayed byte for byte from nine authentic files; the Antalya published hash is unchanged. Both remain experimental retrospective analyses, with no independently verified negative observation population or fire outcomes. Spatial recurrence is the next descriptive data slice; a weather-effect or occurrence model remains ineligible.

The [two-region spatial recurrence audit](research/turkey-spatial-recurrence-2023-2025.md) verifies the same six original NASA file hashes, streams 257,907 country records, and groups 1,419 selected pixels into bounded 0.01° WGS84 research bins. Antalya has **65/248 bins observed on multiple dates** and two unvalidated potential-static review bins; the leading bin contributes **748/1,340** selected pixels on 477 dates. Antalya's FIRMS source type-2 count is **480, 340, 49** in 2023, 2024, 2025, explaining much of the raw count change numerically. Historical type defects and unknown acquisition exposure block a wildfire trend interpretation. Dalaman has four recurrent bins among 34, with no bin meeting the source-based potential-static cue. The browser exposes these aggregates and quality tables; no industrial or wildfire identities are independently classified. The 29,431-byte report replayed byte for byte from authentic files.

`MAX_SCANS=64` and `MAX_PIXELS=1,000,000` remain safeguards for the **old in-memory exploratory model report**. They do not constrain the new `historical_rollup.py` path. Its SQLite study contract hashes the selected nonoverlapping regions and aggregation semantics. Each scan is committed atomically with receipt/asset hashes, source states, selected raw count and distinct cell-hour count. Repeated imports are idempotent; a newly discovered earlier scan or changed first-capture history requires an explicit rebuild from the raw pixel archive. A building study is not queryable. Schema version 1 is checked and unknown versions fail closed; changes to stored semantics require a documented migration/rebuild, never silent reinterpretation. Keep the SQLite database and original assets outside Git, backed up together with their receipt manifest. The compact static aggregate alone cannot restore original pixels. SQLite remains the only analytical store; no DuckDB, Parquet, Workers KV or new hosted service was needed.

**Retention and recovery policy for this preview:** retain each original hashed release asset and the matching SQLite file outside Git for as long as a published aggregate cites it. Back up originals and SQLite together, with the manifest and report SHA-256. Do not purge an input while its report remains a reproducibility claim. Recreate rollup tables with `--rebuild` only after original pixel receipts have been reverified; this does not recreate missing raw pixels. There is no unattended cloud retention/restore service yet, so the archival durability milestone remains experimental. Static public aggregate additions require a size review before commit and are versioned rather than overwritten after an upstream revision.

On the real verified 357,447-pixel SQLite archive, the Batman 25 km rollup processed five receipts in **1.986 s**, peak RSS **33,440 KiB**; a final-code three-date hourly/source-provenance query took **0.0014 s** in a separate process. The rollup found 125 raw pixels and 56 candidate cell-hours in 2026-10-09–11; no date was zero-filled. The final-code **synthetic performance fixture** exercised 1,000,001 records (457,998,336-byte DB, peak RSS 36,460 KiB) and 2,000,001 records (917,209,088-byte DB, peak RSS 121,152 KiB). For the 2M fixture, the first two scan rollup took 4.596 s, remaining three 6.173 s, repeat 0.042 s and bounded date/source-provenance query 0.067 s. These rows test scaling only; they are not fire observations. Measurements are local one-run evidence, not a cloud cost or throughput guarantee. Bounded `fetchmany(2000)` limits Python row buffering; the measured RSS still includes SQLite caches and fixture generation.

Re-run the scale test on a machine with at least 1 GiB temporary disk available (the output is a disposable synthetic SQLite fixture):

```sh
python -m scripts.benchmark_historical_rollup \
  --db /private/rollup-scale-fixture.sqlite3 --rows 2000001
```

The fixture builder bypasses original-asset integrity checks and is **only a performance test**. It is excluded from scientific analyses and public artifacts. CI uses small deterministic integrity fixtures; it does not allocate a 917 MB database on every PR.

The [Batman research report](research/batman-station-thermal-context-2026-10.md) records a genuine NOAA GHCNh 2026 fixed-station source file (SHA-256 `1dcc60fcae10b9552f833169f7dc3284ac63a75398c24596a94d83397dbdf696`) and its historical [FIRMS–station joined JSON](../public/data/batman-weather-context-2026-10-11.json). There are 19,783 annual station rows, 150 in the selected three-date window, 125 FIRMS raw pixels, 56 candidate cell-hours and six distinct candidate hours. Four candidate hours matched prior station temperature/humidity within 60 minutes; two were unmatched after the source file ended. Mean station temperature across 150 records was 17.467 °C; median at the four matched candidate hours was 24.7 °C. The latter is outcome-selected, time-of-day confounded descriptive context. No correlation coefficient, regression, exposure denominator or wildfire rate is supported. First publication/availability of individual weather records is unknown, so none are allowed as historical forecast features. The web page shows a variable-selectable station chart, candidate-hour table and provenance warnings.

The [Sacramento report](research/sacramento-station-thermal-context-2026-10.md) reuses the same pipeline and adds a second real GHCNh station/year file (SHA-256 `2e63cb3f7ea2ca89eb52947071bd8c18cc9e4da80df5b73c808d4f25f14f5a92`) and [joined JSON](../public/data/sacramento-weather-context-2026-10-11.json). Its 25 km circle contains **22 raw pixels, 10 candidate cell-hours and six candidate UTC hours**; five have prior station temperature, wind speed and humidity. The source has 7,221 annual rows but only 51 in the selected dates and stops at 2026-10-11 02:53 UTC. The station temperature mean is 21.612 °C across those 51 rows; the five matched candidate-hour median is 27.8 °C. Site selection followed inspection of thermal observations and station availability; these values do not support a temperature association or cross-region fire-rate ranking. The browser now selects either pilot and displays separate matched-hour/missingness denominators.

The existing next-scan model remains executable with five scans, but its **scientific comparison status is ineligible** even if future targets become variable. The updated report separates source availability, target variability, sample adequacy, temporal/geographic validation, acquisition opportunity, outcome validity, scientific comparison and operational prediction. The old eight-row/five-scan controls are mechanical safeguards only, not universal sample-size thresholds. The real target still has constant training outcomes and zero coefficients; reported MAEs measure collection-output residuals, not fire forecast skill. Acquisition schedules, rolling bootstrap, delayed source data and first-capture deduplication can all change this target. No new model was trained on the Batman data: only four matched candidate hours and no verified negative acquisition windows or independent outcomes exist.

Satellite opportunity remains `unknown`. A successful FIRMS retrieval, a blank archive hour and a missing station measurement are different states. The bounded [NASA CMR geolocation audit](research/batman-viirs-granule-catalog-2026-10.md) uses the NOAA-20/21 375 m geolocation products (`VJ103IMG_NRT`, `VJ203IMG_NRT`, version 2.1). The [paired product audit](research/batman-viirs-paired-product-catalog-2026-10.md) adds public active-fire L2 catalogs (`VJ114IMG_NRT`, `VJ214IMG_NRT`, version 2). The Batman box has 20 exact L1/L2 interval pairs, and 5/7 archived provider-minute groups lie inside those intervals. Catalog metadata alone cannot verify cell-level swath/quality or inspected active-fire product completeness, so no empty hour is promoted to a negative. Distinguish provider failure/defer, no archived candidate, acquisition confirmed with no reported detection, acquisition unavailable, delayed publication and unknown before any occurrence model. The current pilot proves only archived positive thermal candidates.

## Data contract and reproducibility

| Field | Meaning | Availability/limitation |
|---|---|---|
| `observed_at` | Original UTC FIRMS acquisition | Original source row; not ignition time. |
| `first_captured_at`, `first_scan_at` | First local collector receipt and source scan | Must be after observation; used to prevent later data entering earlier transitions. |
| `receipt_sha256`, asset hashes | Source evidence and exact archived bytes | Validated during import/sync. |
| `source_asset_manifest` | Hash, scan and parsed row count of every pixel asset | Pinned in the aggregate; second original-asset import must reproduce it. |
| `source_states` | Collector request outcome per sensor | `complete` does not verify overpass or cloud-free visibility. |
| `new_raw_pixels` | Unique original source records first captured by a scan | Sensor duplicates remain distinct raw records. |
| `new_candidate_cell_hours` | Distinct 0.05° WGS84 cell × observed UTC hour first captured by a scan/region | Both sensors in the same cell-hour count once; not fire count, area, or severity. |
| `daily_rows`, `weekly_rows`, `monthly_rows` | Descriptive sums by original observation time | Every period is flagged `archive_period_complete=false`; missing dates are not zero-filled. |
| `rolling_3_observed_days` | Three-day mean only for contiguous listed UTC dates | Descriptive only; daily coverage is incomplete. |
| Environment variables, agency outcomes | Not included in this pilot | Unit, spatial/time resolution, publication/issue time, availability and missingness contracts must precede integration. |
| `catalog_granules`, `candidate_provider_minutes` | Separate CMR geolocation metadata versus archived FIRMS acquisition-minute groups | Search-box and exact time comparison only; the NetCDF geolocation/quality arrays were not read. `observation_opportunity_verified=false`. |
| `paired_intervals`, `candidate_minutes_with_both_catalogs` | Exact same-platform interval pairing of CMR geolocation L1 and active-fire L2 catalog entries | Product catalog availability context only; L1/L2 file contents and cell quality are uninspected. No negative exposure denominator. |

Reproduce from already downloaded, verified public research releases (no secret needed after download):

```sh
python -m fire_public.release_sync \
  --assets-dir /private/firms-releases --db /private/firms-pixels.sqlite3 \
  --since 2026-10-12 --max-releases 2 --max-total-mb 160 \
  --hours 72 --coverage-json /private/sync-evidence.json
python -m fire_public.thermal_capture_analytics \
  --pixel-db /private/firms-pixels.sqlite3 \
  --regions-json examples/thermal-capture-regions.json \
  --holdout-region AUSTRALIA_NORTH --holdout-region BRAZIL_AMAZON \
  --verify-assets-dir /private/firms-releases \
  --output-json /private/thermal-pilot.json
```

The CLI refuses to overwrite an existing report and does not make a network request. `--verify-assets-dir` independently re-imports the original release assets into a temporary SQLite DB and requires an identical full report. CI tests this mechanism on source fixtures and checks the committed aggregate's internal hash/denominators; CI does not download the 123 MB original archive on every PR. Public GitHub releases can change or expire; a later rerun must compare the report hash, receipt hashes, source versions and record counts before interpreting any numerical difference. No random seed is needed for deterministic one-feature OLS. The model uses Python standard library only; no ML package or runtime is added to the public app.

## Release and decision gates

Research preview permits descriptive archived observations and explicitly experimental collection-output experiments. A validated fire model needs a preregistered target/horizon, separately evidenced positives and negatives, verified acquisition exposure, two-reviewer adjudication, frozen source versions, temporal/geographic/event holdouts, baseline comparison, uncertainty, calibration and prospective monitoring. None has passed for this pilot. The public page therefore shows descriptive counts and research method, with no fire probability or operational prediction layer.

Next large vertical slice: secure a longer original-source archive outside Git and a predeclared second period. NASA LANCE requires an existing Earthdata login/download token for the original NOAA-20/21 NetCDF geolocation and active-fire granules; the bounded anonymous GET reached login HTML, not data. The L1/L2 catalog pairing reduces product-discovery uncertainty but does not establish a cell observation. With authorized granule access, inspect quality and product completeness for a predeclared region-time unit before any no-detection denominator. The second real station pilot is now implemented, but both sites have only five or fewer matched candidate hours and no verified exposure; defer weather regressions and model comparisons until the effective sample and independent evidence improve. Preserve a separate independent fire-label evaluation track.
